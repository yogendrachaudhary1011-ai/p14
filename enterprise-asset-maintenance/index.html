Background

Complex software becomes difficult when users must remember which actions are available, who owns the next step, and what each status means.

My approach is to make those rules visible in the interface instead of leaving them inside documentation or user memory.

That means treating states, actions, summaries, confirmations, and handoffs as one connected system.

This is especially important in dense business software, service platforms, and any flow where an action changes what happens next.

The goal is not to remove necessary complexity, but to organize it so people can make the right decision with less guesswork.

Problem & Goals

Across my work, I use workflow structure to connect information, current state, available action, and system feedback.

01
Actions change with status

The same object can allow different actions when it is draft, submitted, approved, paid, cancelled, completed, or locked.

02
Ownership must be visible

People need to know who owns the next step and whether they can act, review, wait, or recover.

03
Dense screens still need hierarchy

Lists, details, summaries, filters, balances, and notices must remain scannable even when the underlying data is complex.

04
Irreversible actions need friction

Closing, deleting, resetting, reversing, or changing security-sensitive data should be deliberate and visibly confirmed.

GoalCreate workflows where current state, next action, ownership, and consequences are visible at the moment a decision is made.

Key Users

Each role submits, coordinates, executes, or monitors the part of facility maintenance relevant to their work.

01 / REQUEST SUBMISSIONOperator

Creates and updates records while needing clear validation, status, and next steps.

02 / DEPARTMENT SUPPORTReviewer

Checks information, resolves exceptions, and needs context before changing state.

03 / CENTRAL OVERSIGHTAdministrator

Manages access, settings, migrations, fiscal periods, and other high-impact controls.

04 / SITE OPERATIONSCustomer / Service user

Follows booking, order, payment, or service status and needs consistent feedback.

05 / FIELD EXECUTIONDeveloper handoff

Receives structured design decisions, component states, and interaction behavior for implementation.

Approach

I translate requirements into connected states and task flows before committing to screen-level UI.

Define scenarios

List the real tasks, edge cases, and irreversible actions the product must support.

Map roles & ownership

Clarify who can view, edit, approve, reverse, cancel, or complete each state.

Structure states & actions

Group information and actions by status so users see what matters now instead of every possible control.

Design & collaborate

Turn flows into reusable UI patterns and communicate behavior clearly during implementation.

Interface foundationLists, details, forms, summaries, statuses, dialogs, and reusable feedback patterns.
Interaction logicKey states and transitions designed to show what changed, what remains, and what can happen next.
Workflow structureA workflow should make the path from input to decision to outcome understandable without relying on hidden rules.

Solution & Outcomes

The resulting interface patterns make complex workflows easier to follow without oversimplifying the product rules behind them.

01 / Workflow

State-aware actions

Actions are filtered by current status so the interface shows what is valid now and avoids presenting controls that cannot be used.

Design outcome:users can understand what state they are in, what action is available, and what will change after they act.
02 / Planning

Visible ownership

Ownership, reviewer state, and responsibility are surfaced so users know whether to act or wait.

Design outcome:handoffs become easier to follow without separate explanation.
03 / Execution

Guarded high-risk actions

Reversal, reset, deletion, closing, and account-security actions use deliberate confirmation and clear consequence messaging.

Design outcome:critical changes become harder to trigger accidentally and easier to understand before confirmation.
04 / Oversight

Consistent feedback

Success, error, pending, empty, locked, and completed states use consistent messaging and visual hierarchy across modules.

Design outcome:users receive predictable feedback instead of having to infer whether an action worked.
