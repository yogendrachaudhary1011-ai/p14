SOMTU
A case study on designing the complete School of Management, Tribhuvan University website, from landing page to registration, across desktop and mobile, as a design intern at Aarawan Media & Tech.
The School of Management at Tribhuvan University (SOMTU) is a Kathmandu-based management school. I designed its public website: how people discover programs, apply, read notices, join events, explore research, and find official documents.

THE PROBLEM
SOMTU serves prospective students, current students, faculty, and visitors. Its programs, notices, research, resources, and registration need a clear structure so each audience can find what matters and decide what to do next.
Applicants need a clear path
Which program fits, and when does it close?
Long application forms are easy to abandon
Admission status must be easy to trust
Students return for tasks
Exam notices, results, and official documents
Searching a long list is slow
Most visits happen on a phone
The school needs credibility
Research, events, and news must feel current
Official content in Nepali and English
One consistent brand across every page
MY CHALLENGE
Design one connected website where every audience can reach their task in a few taps, on desktop and mobile, while keeping the School of Management’s brand consistent across more than ten feature areas.
Constraints:
Many content types: programs, news, notices, events, research, forms, documents
An established blue and orange brand to extend, not replace
Real content, long lists, and PDF-based notices
Two target sizes, 1440 px and 428 px, designed as equals
The aha moment - insights
Consistency is emotional, not functional.
People come to do one thing.
Apply, check a result, find a notice. Every page should make its main action obvious.
Lists are the website.
News, notices, events, research, and documents are all lists, so one search and filter pattern can serve them all.
Long forms need a map.
A stepper, a review step, and a confirmation turn a heavy application into a guided path.
KEY DECISIONS & WHY?
One shell for every page
Constraint:
More than ten feature areas, each with its own audience.
What I did:
Built a shared header: an announcement bar for live admissions news, utility links for SOMAT Result, Resources, Research, and Publications, a main menu with dropdowns, and a persistent Apply Now button.
Why
People always know where they are and where the next action is, and every page inherits the same structure.
A landing page that leads with two actions
Constraint:
A university home page can say everything and help no one.
What I did:
A full-width “Knowledge Without Borders” hero with Explore Programs and Apply Now, then programs, milestones, and faculty.
Why
Browsers and ready-to-apply visitors both get an obvious first tap.
Program pages from one template
Constraint:
A university home page can say everything and help no one.
What I did:
A full-width “Knowledge Without Borders” hero with Explore Programs and Apply Now, then programs, milestones, and faculty.
Why
Browsers and ready-to-apply visitors both get an obvious first tap.
Updates and notices as two different experiences
Problem:
Stories are browsed, notices are looked up.
What I did:
Two tabs with shared search and filters. Updates lead with a featured story. Notices use date-first cards, category chips with counts, and a mobile filter sheet. Notice pages add status, dates, Share, and Download PDF.
Why
Each content type gets the layout that suits how it is read.
Event pages that change with the event
What I did:
Designed detail pages for upcoming, registered, ended, and completed events, with status card such as “This event has not started yet” and a separate registration page.
Why
Visitors see the right action at the right time instead of a button that no longer works.
Registration as a guided path
Problem:
An admission application asks for a lot of information.
What I did:
Split it into seven steps (general details, academic report, financial support, referees, Why SOMTU, required documents, review and submit) with a numbered stepper, the selected program beside the form, and a confirmation with an application ID and help contacts.
Why
People always see how far they are, can check everything before sending, and finish with proof that it worked.
Event pages that change with the event
What I did:
Designed detail pages for upcoming, registered, ended, and completed events, with status card such as “This event has not started yet” and a separate registration page.
Why
Visitors see the right action at the right time instead of a button that no longer works.
Research that reads like a publication
What I did:
A featured study, filters for publications, research projects, working projects, and case studies, a sort control, pagination, and detail pages with Download PDF and an accordion for long content on mobile.
Why
Academic content gets credibility and depth without overwhelming a small screen.
SOLUTION
Six ideas held the whole website together.
One shell
Shared header, footer, and Apply Now on every page.
Action-first pages
Each page makes its main action obvious.
One list pattern
Search, chips, and pagination reused for news, notices,
events, research, and resources.
Guided forms
Steppers, review, and
confirmation for long tasks.
State-aware screens
Upcoming, ended, empty, and success states designed, not left to chance.
Mobile as an equal
Every chapter designed at 428 px with its own patterns.
Why this approach works?
Familiar everywhere
Learn one page and you know the rest.
Faster tasks
Search and filters reach any item in a few taps.
Easier to build
Repeated patterns and a documented library cut handoff questions.
but.. WHAT NEEDED WORK?
First Attempt:
Show every category chip on mobile, as on desktop.
Problem
Seven chips with counts overflowed a 428 px screen and pushed results out of sight.
Fix:
Moved the full set into a Filter Content sheet with sort, reset, and apply, and kept the main screen focused on results.
Learning:
Desktop patterns do not shrink. They have to be redesigned for thumbs.
DESIGN SYSTEM
I extended the School of Management’s blue and orange brand into a system for content-heavy pages: a campus-photo banner under the header, white cards on a pale surface, and orange reserved for actions. All 81 component variants are documented.
THE PRODUCT
Eleven feature areas, each designed for desktop and mobile. Scroll inside any frame to see the full screen.
LEARNINGS
Systems beat pages
A shared shell and list pattern made ten feature areas feel like one product.
Design every state
Upcoming, ended, empty, and success states are part of the experience.
Match layout to reading
Stories, notices, and research each needed their own layout.
Mobile is its own design
Filters, forms, and long pages needed new patterns, not smaller ones.
WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY

Run quick tests on applying and finding a notice.
Document rationale
Write short decision notes beside each chapter.
Plan for language
Design an English and Nepali toggle for notices and forms.
