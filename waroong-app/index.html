Background

Numira brings business operations into one software environment. My work focused on making dense workflows easier to scan, understand, and operate without hiding important financial or administrative states.

Business operations

Complex workflows need visible structure.

Customers, suppliers, purchases, bills, payments, vouchers, accounts, fiscal years, users, and settings all carry different states, actions, and dependencies.

The design challenge was to keep those relationships understandable while preserving the detail required by accounting and business-management workflows.

Party records Search, filter, open details, update, and recover records within a consistent party-management flow.
Purchase status Review transaction details and prepare the next action.
Accounting & control Maintain accounting records and administrative settings with explicit safeguards.

Problem & Goals

Each module needed to show what exists, what state it is in, what can happen next, and what should never happen accidentally.

01

Make party management easier to scan

Customer and supplier lists need search, filters, detail pages, groups, pricing levels, and a clear recovery path for deleted records.

02

Clarify purchase states and next actions

Purchase orders, bills, supplier payments, debit notes, and write-offs need status-aware actions that match the current state.

03

Protect accounting accuracy

Vouchers use reversal rather than deletion, while the chart of accounts needs a clear hierarchy of account types and groups.

04

Make high-risk settings deliberate

Fiscal-year closing, data migration, reset, user access, subscription, and security actions need clear confirmation and safeguards.

Design goal

Clarity with control

Create dense, structured workflows that remain clear, traceable, and safe to operate across everyday and high-risk actions.

Key Users

The product serves different business tasks, so the interface needs to support people managing parties, purchases, accounting records, and administrative settings without changing mental models between modules.

Business user

Needs dense information to stay readable while actions remain predictable, reversible where appropriate, and explicit when they are irreversible.

Approach

I organized each module around its states, available actions, information hierarchy, and the decisions a user needs to make before moving forward.

Checkpoint 01

Structure

Organized dense information into lists, details, forms, summaries, and confirmation patterns.

UI & reusable patterns

Developer collaboration

Designed Solutions

The final design direction focused on findability, visible status, accounting accuracy, and deliberate control over irreversible actions.

01

Party management

Searchable customer and supplier lists, detail pages, customer levels, groups, add flows, and recovery for deleted records.

Design outcome

People can find parties quickly, understand ownership and pricing context, and recover records within a controlled window.

Search→Details→Groups→Recovery
02

Purchase & payment states

Purchase lists, status-based menus, bills, supplier payments, debit notes, write-offs, and clear partial-payment summaries.

Design outcome

Each transaction state exposes only the actions that make sense and keeps remaining balances visible.

Orders→Status→Balance
03

Accounting controls

Voucher entry and reversal, chart-of-accounts hierarchy, fiscal-year management, and guarded closing flows.

Design outcome

Corrections stay traceable and high-risk accounting actions receive deliberate confirmation.

Voucher→Reversal→Closing
04

Settings & security

Data migration, users and roles, subscription, sessions, password changes, backup/restore, and reset flows designed with visible safeguards.

Design outcome

Critical settings remain understandable and harder to trigger by accident.
